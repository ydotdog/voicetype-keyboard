## Changes in this candidate

- Recordings interrupted during transcription are retained privately on the
  device for explicit retry or discard. Retry preserves the recording's audio,
  duration, and request UUID, including after relaunch. Expired authentication
  hides the saved recording until its owner signs in again; explicit sign-out,
  account deletion, or switching to another owner clears it.
- Recording start, stop, audio interruption, background expiration, and keyboard
  session transitions now guard against stale or overlapping work. Configured
  session limits are enforced, individual clips are capped, and finishing the
  microphone session can let the current transcription finish.
- The keyboard handles pending commands and unavailable app-opening requests,
  uses the supported keyboard-switching path, and no longer accesses
  `UIApplication` through responder-chain/runtime bypasses. Recording controls,
  purchase availability, and retry/error states have been clarified.
- Account and StoreKit responses are tied to the initiating account session.
  Stale responses cannot restore a signed-out account or overwrite a different
  account. Purchase submissions reject wrong-account, revoked, or unknown
  transactions and finish only after backend confirmation.
- The backend persists per-user `Idempotency-Key` claims and recording
  fingerprints. A completed retry returns its original transcript and charge
  with the current balance. Concurrent duplicates receive retry guidance; stale
  workers cannot debit after another attempt takes ownership.
- Credit holds are released on failures, malformed provider responses,
  cancellation, and completion. Expired holds cannot spend funds reserved by
  another request. Upload/field bounds, verified-user rate limits, JWT claims,
  and fail-closed StoreKit verification have been tightened.
- Apple authorization-code exchange is bound to the verified Apple identity.
  Configured production sign-in requires a newly exchanged or previously stored
  revocable grant. Failed Apple revocation preserves the account for retry.
  Server conflict details are displayed instead of treating every HTTP 409 as
  an in-progress recording.

## Rollback compatibility

The database change adds `transcription_requests`; it does not rename or remove
existing columns. The old backend can operate with this additional table left
in place, so a code rollback requires **no reverse SQL migration**. Retain the
table and its completed request records.

The old backend does not enforce transcription idempotency. Rolling back to it
while clients can retry saved recordings may reintroduce duplicate charges.
Keep idempotency enforcement available for retry-capable clients; schema
compatibility alone does not make that behavioral rollback safe.
